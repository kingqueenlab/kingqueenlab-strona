Dual receptor agonist

Tirzepatide / LY3298176

What is Tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide is a synthetic peptide that activates both the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR) and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R). PubChem describes it as a 39-amino-acid linear peptide conjugated to a C20 fatty diacid moiety.

GIPR Glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor
GLP-1R Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor

Mechanistic context

Why two receptors?

GIP and GLP-1 are incretin hormones involved in glucose-dependent signalling. Tirzepatide was designed as a single molecule with activity at both receptor systems.

Safety context

Trials must be read in full.

In SURMOUNT-1, gastrointestinal events were the most frequently reported adverse events and were mostly mild to moderate, occurring primarily during dose escalation. Safety conclusions require the full protocol, population and regulated product context.

Regulatory context

Approved active ingredient.

Tirzepatide is the active ingredient in regulated medicines in specified jurisdictions. A research material should never be assumed to be equivalent to an approved pharmaceutical product.

Triple receptor agonist

Retatrutide / LY3437943

What is Retatrutide?

Retatrutide is a single synthetic peptide studied as an agonist at three receptor systems: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. Published research therefore describes it as a triple-hormone-receptor agonist.

GIPR Incretin signalling
GLP-1R Incretin signalling
GCGR Glucagon receptor

Mechanistic context

Why three receptors?

The research rationale combines two incretin receptor pathways with glucagon receptor activity in one peptide. The contribution of each pathway must be interpreted within experimental and clinical-study design.

Evidence maturity

Investigational context matters.

Phase 2 findings can guide later research but are not a substitute for completed regulatory review. Current conclusions may change as larger and longer trials report.

Material distinction

No equivalence assumption.

Published clinical outcomes concern trial-specific material, manufacturing and monitoring. They cannot be transferred to independently supplied research material.
